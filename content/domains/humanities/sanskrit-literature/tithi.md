# tithi

A **tithi** is a lunar day defined by the Moon's angular distance from the Sun. The distance increases by about 12 degrees for each tithi, so one lunar month contains 30 tithis.

The basic calculation is:

~~~
angular distance = (lunar longitude - solar longitude) modulo 360
tithi number = floor(angular distance / 12) + 1
~~~

Tithis 1–15 belong to the bright half, or śukla pakṣa. Tithis 16–30 belong to the dark half, or [[krishna]] pakṣa; subtracting 15 gives the number within that half.

This calculation identifies the angular segment. A working pañcāṅga also applies local time, sunrise rules, and the relevant astronomical data. Nakṣatra and pāda are separate calculations.
