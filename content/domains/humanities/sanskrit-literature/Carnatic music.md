# Carnatic music

South Indian classical music. Rāga (melody) + tāla (rhythm cycle).

## Svara

- Seven: sa ri ga ma pa da ni.
- 12 positions in the octave. sa and pa are fixed; ri, ga, ma, da, ni have variants (ri1, ri2, ...).

## Rāga

- Ārohaṇa (ascent) + avarohaṇa (descent) + the gamakas (oscillations) that give it character.
- Melakarta = parent rāga, all 7 svaras both ways. 72 of them.
  - 12 chakras × 6 rāgas.
  - 1–36 use śuddha madhyama (ma1), 37–72 prati madhyama (ma2).
  - The name encodes the number → [[Katapayadi_Melakarta_Guide]]
- Janya = derived rāga. Drops or reorders svaras of a melakarta.

## Tāla

- Repeating beat cycle. Most common: Ādi tāla = 8 beats.
- 35-tāla system: 7 sūlādi tālas × 5 jātis.

## Forms

- Varṇam: practice and concert opener.
- Kṛti: pallavi → anupallavi → caraṇam. Example: [[Bantu reethi koluvu]].
- Also kīrtana, tillāna.

## People

- Purandara Dāsa (16th c.): graded lessons for learners. Called the pitāmaha (grandfather) of Carnatic music.
- Trinity (18th–19th c.): [[tyagaraja swamy]], Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri.
